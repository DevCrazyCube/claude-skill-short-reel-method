**Truth:** A coat holds a library seat for an absent owner.
**We see:** A student with a laptop walks along a row of desks. One chair has a coat on it and nobody in it. They look at the next chair: a coat. And the next.
**Turn:** The frame widens: the whole hall is coats on chairs, and the student is the only person in it.
**Reaction:** The student stands still in the middle, laptop under one arm, looking at the camera.
**Why it had to be so:** Everyone reserves a seat and nobody uses it, so a full library is an empty one.
**Caption:** Fully booked.

## Cold read

Run 1, a fresh agent, two messages. Its answers, unedited.

After message 1 (the hook and "We see"):

> A student arrives at the library at 9:00 looking for a desk, but every seat is already "reserved" by a coat
> with no person in it.
>
> 1. The camera pulls back to show the whole library full of coats and not a single person studying.
> 2. The student gives up and sits on the floor (or stairs, or by the toilets) with the laptop.
> 3. The student finally finds one free chair, then puts their own coat on it and walks off for coffee, becoming
>    one of them.

After message 2 (the turn):

> Everyone came in early, claimed a desk with their coat and left again, so the library is "full" without anyone
> actually studying there.
>
> Maybe, leaning yes: to the friend who always drops their coat on a chair at 8:30 and vanishes for coffee, or to
> the study-group chat during exam weeks. The truth is recognisable, but the widening only confirms what the
> first three coats already told me (it was my first guess), so it lands as a nod more than a laugh.

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| The situation | pass: it is the truth |
| The three guesses | **fail: guess 1 is the turn** |
| The reason | pass: it is the reason above |
| Passing it on | fail: "maybe" is not a yes |

**Stopped.** Keep the truth, drop the turn. The reader's third guess, the student who finds the one free chair,
puts a coat on it and leaves, is a better turn than the one pitched: it comes from the character, not from the
camera. It goes back in as a new pitch, to a new reader.

This pitch passes every counted check and still fails. That is the point of the example.
